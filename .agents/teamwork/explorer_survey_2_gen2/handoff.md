## 2. Logic Chain
- *Step 1 (Observation 1 & 2):* Edge devices (`t3.micro`, 2 vCPU, 1 GiB RAM) cannot allocate significant CPU or memory to continuous packet-level inspection without starving the primary service. Standard packet IDSs (Suricata) process every packet ($O(R_{\text{pps}})$), whereas periodic socket sampling ($O(1)$ at 1 Hz) decouples monitoring overhead from attack rate.
- *Step 2 (Observation 2):* Linux exposes socket connection states via `/proc/net/tcp` (ASCII hex tables for states `01` ESTABLISHED, `03` SYN_RECV, queue depths, and remote IPs) and protocol engine metrics via `/proc/net/snmp` (`PassiveOpens`, `AttemptFails`) and `/proc/net/netstat` (`ListenDrops`, `SyncookiesSent`, `TCPReqQFullDrop`). Shelling out to `ss -tan` incurs repetitive `fork()`/`execve()` and pipe IPC overhead (taking 8–22 ms and 1.5–3.0% CPU), whereas an in-process Python reader achieves median sample costs $< 2.5\text{ ms}$ and CPU $< 0.5\%$.
- *Step 3 (Observation 2):* Without attacker RST suppression (`iptables -A OUTPUT -p tcp --tcp-flags RST RST -d $VICTIM_PRIVATE_IP -j DROP`), the attacker kernel automatically emits TCP RSTs upon receiving unsolicited SYN-ACKs generated by `hping3` raw packets. This tears down half-open sockets immediately on the victim, artificially neutralizing the flood. Under the `break` profile (`tcp_syncookies = 0`, queue 128), half-open sockets accumulate and block incoming SYNs. Under the `fix` profile (`tcp_syncookies = 1`), half-open sockets do not accumulate (`syn_recv \approx 0`), necessitating secondary detection signals on cookie counters ($\Delta \text{SyncookiesSent}$) and queue pressure.
- *Step 4 (Observation 2):* To prevent false alarms during high-volume legitimate traffic, the detector must distinguish flash crowds from SYN floods. During flash crowds, connection attempts complete rapidly ($C_t \ge 0.85$); during SYN floods, handshakes remain incomplete ($C_t \rightarrow 0.0$). The dual condition $\text{Trigger} = (x_t > \max(\text{FLOOR}, K \cdot \mu_t)) \land (C_t < \tau)$ guarantees zero false alarms on flash crowds. Freezing the adaptive baseline $\mu_t$ while in alarm prevents long-duration floods from normalizing the attack state into the moving average.
- *Step 5 (Observation 1 & 2):* For connection exhaustion attacks (`conn_hold.py`), handshakes complete successfully ($C_t \approx 1.0$), blinding D1. Detector D2 resolves this by evaluating established connection growth rate ($G_t$) and source concentration share ($S_t$).
- *Step 6 (Observation 1 & 2):* Strict separation of parameter tuning (reps 1–3) from final evaluation (reps 4–5) prevents data snooping and overfitting.

## 3. Caveats
1. **AWS VPC Anti-Spoofing Invariant:** AWS hypervisors enforce anti-spoofing drop rules. Consequently, all SYN flood traffic originates from the attacker's true private IP address. While this limitation is documented as a threat to validity, it does not alter the fundamental half-open queue exhaustion mechanics on the victim.
2. **Synthetic Traffic Distributions:** Flash crowds simulated by `ab` and background loads by `wrk` represent Poisson/concurrency models rather than real human browsing behavior (e.g. assets, think time, keep-alive reuse).
3. **Burstable CPU Credits:** `t3.micro` instances operate under credit limits. Running prolonged attack benchmarks without adequate cooldown could trigger CPU credit throttling, artificially distorting probe latencies.
4. **Single Kernel Distribution:** Findings are framed around Ubuntu 24.04 LTS (Linux kernel 6.8). Differences in TCP stack implementation across older Linux kernels, BSD, or Windows are not empirically modeled.
